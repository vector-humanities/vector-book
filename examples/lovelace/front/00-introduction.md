# Introduction

In 1840 Charles Babbage went to Turin for the meeting of Italian scientists, and on several mornings explained the Analytical Engine to a group of them in his rooms. One of the group was Luigi Federico Menabrea, then an officer of the military engineers, who "collected the materials" for an account of the engine during those meetings [@babbage1864, 135–36]. His *Notions sur la machine analytique de M. Charles Babbage* appeared in the *Bibliothèque universelle de Genève* in October 1842 [@menabrea1842].

Ada Lovelace translated the memoir into English. Babbage recalled that when she told him of the translation he asked why she had not written an original paper, and then suggested that she add notes to Menabrea's text instead [@babbage1864, 136].[^ed-i1] The translation, with seven Notes signed A.A.L., was printed in the third volume of Richard Taylor's *Scientific Memoirs* in 1843 [@lovelace1843]. Babbage put the Notes at "about three times the length of the original memoir" [@babbage1864, 136].

The memoir describes the engine from the outside: its two parts, the *store* of columns that hold numbers and the *mill* that operates on them, and the punched cards that direct both, adapted from the cards of the Jacquard loom (Figure 1). It works through a pair of simultaneous equations to show how a calculation is laid out for the machine. The Notes go further in two directions. Notes A, B and E set out what the engine is: a machine for operations on symbols, whose cards can be repeated in *cycles* and *cycles of cycles*, and whose results need not be numbers. Notes D and G set out how a calculation is written for it, in diagrams that follow every Variable through every operation. Note G ends with the diagram for computing the Bernoulli numbers, set in this edition on two turned pages.

The correspondence between Lovelace and Babbage during the writing of the Notes has been read in several ways, and the share of each in the Notes has been disputed; @fuegi2003 give an account drawn from the letters. On Lovelace's mathematical training, which her correspondence with Augustus De Morgan documents in detail, see @hollings2017 and @hollings2018. @swade2000 gives the history of Babbage's engines and of the Difference Engine built from his drawings in the twentieth century, and @schaffer1994 places the engines in the factory system and the debates about intelligence and labour of the period.[^ed-i2]
