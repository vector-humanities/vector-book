# Note on the Text

The text is that of the first printing, in *Scientific Memoirs* 3 (1843), pages 666–731, taken from the Wikisource transcription, which has been validated against the page images.[^ed-t1] The numbers in the outer margin give the pages of that printing; where a page begins inside a table, its number is set beside the first line after the table. The memoir is preceded, as in 1843, by the list of printed papers on Babbage's engines with which the editor of *Scientific Memoirs* introduced it.

Spelling, capitals and punctuation are those of 1843, including "œconomy", "Bernouilli" in Babbage's quotation, and Lovelace's hyphenated "Variable-cards" beside "Working Variables". Words set in small capitals at the opening of the memoir and the Notes are set here in full capitals.

There are two series of footnotes. Lovelace's footnotes to the memoir and to her Notes are numbered with the editor's and set without a prefix. The editor's footnotes begin "Ed." Footnotes that Lovelace placed in the cells of her tables are set in the tables, numbered in the same series.

Lovelace's tables and diagrams are set from the transcription. The two widest, the diagram belonging to Note D and the diagram for the computation of the Bernoulli numbers in Note G, are set on pages turned through ninety degrees. In 1843 the Note D diagram was printed on its own page, turned. Figure 2 shows the Note D diagram as it was printed, with editorial glosses over its three groups of Variables.

![The diagram belonging to Note D as printed in 1843, turned to read across the page.[^ed-t2] Key: the glosses in italic give later terms for Lovelace's three groups of Variables; see the Glossary.](figures/note-d-diagram-p711.jpg)

The diagrams have not been corrected. Where one of them is in error, an editor's note says so.
